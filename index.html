Which skills go together, and where can they take you?

We mined the skills listed in LinkedIn job postings for pairs that appear together, grouped them into skill communities, used those communities to see which job roles are alike, and attached salaries to the roles.

Salaries

Salaries come from a second LinkedIn dataset that includes pay. Each posting title was matched to the closest standardized role with sentence embeddings, which connects the pay to the roles and communities of the skill map.

How the map was built

Method

PCY: choosing the support threshold

Lower support keeps more pairs but weakens the bucket filter. At 0.25% the filter removes about 28% of candidate pairs while keeping over 2,000 frequent pairs (10,000-posting pilot).

PCY: number of buckets

The frequent pairs are identical for every bucket count, so buckets only affect efficiency. 250,000 buckets cut the candidates from about 84,500 to 5,500.

Louvain: choosing the resolution

Modularity peaks around resolution 1 to 1.5 with about 15 broad communities. We use resolution 3, where modularity is somewhat lower (0.63 instead of 0.69) but the 24 communities are more specific, for example separating software from web development.

Most frequent skill pairs

Share of postings that list both skills. Frequent pairs are dominated by general skills.

Most associated skill pairs

Lift: how many times more often the pair appears than if the skills were independent (pairs with at least 50 postings).

Limitations

  • The skill map uses a random sample of 25,000 of the 1.26 million postings with skills; the full run uses the same scripts.
  • Skills are extracted from posting text and normalized with a hand-made mapping, so some synonyms remain separate.
  • Salaries come from a different, smaller dataset in which only some states require pay to be listed, and hourly pay is annualized as full time.
  • Roles are matched to posting titles by embedding similarity, so ambiguous titles such as "general manager" may land in the wrong community.
  • Community names are our own interpretation of the skills and roles they contain.
